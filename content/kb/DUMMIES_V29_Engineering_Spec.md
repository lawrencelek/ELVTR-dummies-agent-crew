## 5 The Player Character — mechanics

### 5.2 Inputs, velocity and locomotion

Body stores position and actual world velocity separately from desired direction and facing. A/legs is edge-triggered lunge; B/arms handles cones. Normalise input direction. While moving, translation follows requested direction and facing turns at constant angular rate; misalignment above `movement.alignThreshold` multiplies speed by `movement.turningSpeedFraction`. Ground buildup is linear from `movement.walkSpeed` to `movement.runSpeed` over `movement.rampSeconds`. Reset buildup on stop, counter-brake or lunge. Carry applies `upgrades.carrySpeed` to ground movement only.

A snapshots facing at the start of the input tick, before ordinary steering. Same-tick A takes precedence over B, including when A is denied; an ignored B event does not queue. Denied actions spend no resource. Release coast linearly decays the current velocity over `movement.stopSeconds`. A request more than `movement.counterPressAngle` away from velocity first brakes over `movement.stopSeconds * movement.counterPressStop` while facing turns, then resumes movement from zero buildup. A new non-opposing request cancels release coast. At rest velocity is zero, not an inferred facing speed.

Recovery permits facing and linearly ramps held movement from zero to walk speed over `movement.lungeRecovery`; run buildup remains zero until recovery ends. No new lunge is accepted during recovery. Releasing direction during recovery requests zero velocity. Ordinary turn/walk tuning applies at the next tick before input; it does not deform an accepted lunge snapshot.

| B interaction | Rule |
|---|---|
| No held cone, Carry owned | Press picks up nearest eligible stationary cone in normal movement; reject pickup during lunge/recovery |
| Held cone, Carry only | Press drops immediately |
| Held cone, Throw owned | Tap-release before `input.dropHoldSeconds` throws; holding to that threshold drops once and consumes the gesture |
| Held cone during lunge/recovery | Drop/throw permitted; release snapshots actual velocity at that instant |
| B held after an action | No repeat; release and press again for another gesture |

The default hold threshold is 0.25 s, a trial input choice preserving close drop after buying Throw. At exactly the threshold, drop wins. A player can tap B during the early lunge for a fast throw, or prime B before a lunge; no throw is inferred merely from moving. A cancelled/same-tick-suppressed gesture cannot fire later. Write-off clears its pending state. Teach the two B gestures in the shop and device prompt; allow an accessible separate Drop command mapping without changing mechanics. Human usability remains open.

### 5.3 Lunge, actual velocity and solid response

Accept A only when alive, outside lunge/recovery, with at least `battery.lungeCost` charge. Snapshot reach, facing and duration. Reach is `movement.lungeDistance * max(movement.wearFloor, remainingHealth / damage.bodyHealth) * launchSurfaceLungeFactor`. At the current defaults it is 164 at full health, 82 at half health and 73.8 at the wear floor. These are examples derived from parameters, not independent constants.

The trial distance curve is piecewise linear: 70% of reach in the first one-third of `movement.lungeDuration`, the remainder in the remaining two-thirds. Actual lunge velocity is the derivative of that curve along the current permitted path: at fresh reach it is 1,148 world units/s in the early segment and 246 in the late segment. At an exact segment boundary, the following segment's velocity applies. Within a tick, split swept motion at curve boundaries; evaluate damage from the pre-response velocity at the actual contact time. Throw uses velocity at release, not nominal lunge speed or total distance divided by duration.

Spine alone permits steering on subsequent ticks within its rate and total-angle bounds. Without it, direction cannot change the accepted launch. Static/vehicle collision can truncate translation early; the original action timer and subsequent recovery deadline still run, with no early extra lunge. There is no state-based damage multiplier in V29. Damage depends on actual relative closing motion, so a blocked body's continued lunge animation cannot award an artificial 5× hit.

Trial contact response is kinematic and non-bouncy: resolve position to the swept time of impact with non-overlap, remove inward relative normal velocity, preserve tangent, and inherit only the moving collider's necessary normal push. Use the pre-response velocities for assessment. Normal movement may slide tangentially; a lunge's remaining self-propelled translation ends on its first solid block. A moving vehicle may still push it while its action timer runs. If multiple colliders admit no legal non-overlapping resolution, enter technical containment rather than teleporting or awarding extra impacts. Reference `contact_response` demonstrates the single-normal velocity projection; the actual collision solver and constrained geometry remain engine work.

Recovery begins at the snapshotted lunge deadline and lasts its declared duration, including after early obstruction. Pause/hit-stop freeze these simulation deadlines. T01/T08 must exercise early walls, glancing contacts, stationary vehicles, pushing and two-contact constraints.

### 5.4 Battery

Charge refills continuously at the inverse of `battery.secondsPerCharge`, after `battery.refillDelaySeconds` without a successful spend. Clamp to current capacity. Pauses, reports, purchases and hit-stop freeze simulation clocks. Denied actions do not postpone refill.

Capacity three is not described as exactly three attempts before waiting: recovery and repositioning allow partial or full recharge. Measure denied opportunities and resource decisions in real encounters. Do not make the baseline deliberately tedious to justify future battery upgrades.

A capacity purchase in an expansion profile clamps the current body's charge to the new capacity without refilling it. A subsequent replacement body starts full. The ordering of purchases and body replacement is specified in §§9–10.

### 5.6 The two branches and velocity-based throws

| Upgrade | Immediate effect | Prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| Hips | Faster deliberate reorientation | None |
| Spine | Steer within the original lunge constraints | Hips |
| Carry | Pick up and deliberately place a cone | None |
| Throw | Add a velocity-inheriting ranged release, while retaining close drop | Carry |

Every dummy can punt cones; Carry and Throw must improve real placements over that baseline. Pickup selects the nearest unheld stationary cone within `props.cone.pickupEdgeRange` edge-to-edge distance, tie by prop ID, using full footprint checks. Held cones have a single owner and no free-world collider. Close drop starts at `body.radius + props.cone.radius + props.cone.placementClearance` ahead; current example 19.1 units. Invalid release keeps the cone held.

For a throw let `X = upgrades.throwDistance`, `T = props.cone.throwSeconds`, `f = unit facing`, and `v = actual dummy velocity at release`:

```text
coneLaunchVelocity = v + f * X / T
unobstructedDisplacement = v * T + f * X
```

This is dimensional velocity inheritance, not a full ballistic simulation. Do not add world units/s directly to world units. No automatic 1.5× walk or 3× lunge range bonus is applied; those would be additional arcade boosts, not a consequence of physics. With X=120 and T=0.2, forward velocities 0, 81, 162, 1,148 and 246 give displacements 120, 136.2, 152.4, 349.6 and 169.2 respectively, measured from release position. These velocity examples are inputs; actual ground speed while holding is reduced by Carry, so steady aligned carried walk/run produce 133.77/147.54. Lunge speed is not reduced by Carry. Sideways velocity produces a sideways component; opposite velocity reduces forward travel.

The cone follows its launch vector for T simulation seconds, sweeps its full footprint and stops at the first obstruction, otherwise comes to rest at the endpoint. Collision stopping and the fixed travel period are deliberate arcade simplifications. It cannot tunnel, damage the dummy, score, displace cars or train a model. The released cone keeps its ID. Baseline punting travels `props.cone.puntDistance` over `props.cone.puntSeconds` along contact velocity, under the same swept stop rules, with no repeated in-flight punts. A stationary touch does not punt.

World owns the cone trajectory/range; Body supplies an immutable position/facing/actual-velocity snapshot. Throw-range edits apply only to the next accepted throw; in-flight props retain their snapshot. Cone learning is intentionally absent. Vehicles still sense and avoid cones under ordinary traffic rules. Compare both openings, close drop, fast/slow throws and punt through human playtesting; longer range is not assumed better in every encounter.

### 5.7 Fifteen-node expansion design — Expansion-gated

The five-part body remains the organising metaphor. It is not a literal count of physical degrees of freedom and not a promise that every node is available in the short profile.

| Branch | Foundation | Follow-on option 1 | Follow-on option 2 | Admission condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head: information | Wider view | Memory | Ears | Information must improve observable decisions, not merely expose more decoration |
| Torso: facing and aim | Hips | Spine | Neck | Neck needs direct, accessible aim control and no involuntary movement penalty |
| Legs: traversal | Spring start | Jump | Grip | Authored routes must give the offered ability an advantage over walking around |
| Arms: objects and contact | Carry | Hold on | Throw | Attachment and physical cone handling must be complete for offered nodes; cones never train |
| Battery: action resources | Big battery | Kinetic charge | Fast charge | Measured action pressure and viable purchase sequences must justify them |

**Wider view / Memory / Ears.** Wider view expands resolved sensor information. Memory retains a timestamped last-seen snapshot, visibly stale rather than live knowledge. Ears forecasts a planned entry; a waiting marker replaces a false countdown when admission is blocked. Baseline visible actor/commitment cues remain intact.

**Neck.** The player controls independent aim through a supported mapping, such as an appropriate second directional input. There is no automatic nearest-car lock and no automatic 20% movement penalty. A profile cannot sell Neck on a device without an accessible mapping. Validate deliberately selecting the farther relevant car while moving, then returning to normal facing, before activation.

**Spring start / Grip.** Spring start removes run buildup. Grip removes low-grip slide/extended coast, not all stopping distance. Gravel and puddles can provide low-grip situations; diesel is not claimed to be the only such surface.

**Jump.** A second distinct A press within the jump window can buy a gap hop with a second charge. It keeps the remaining path and adds its worn extension. It does not restart the lunge. All contacts use actual relative closing speed, including airborne contacts; Jump cannot receive a hidden action-state multiplier. It clears flagged floor gaps, not cars, cones or other solids. Landing uses the full footprint. Recovery ends only after both action recovery and landing recovery requirements are satisfied. Spine steering, if combined, ends with the original lunge window. All combination cases need fixtures before offering Jump.

**Hold on.** A valid grab uses an unspent appearance and an ordinary non-lunge side-contact assessment. Its positive impact consumes the normal allowance; surviving contact may attach only at a valid point with safe detachment space. A spends normal lunge charge to launch along facing without inheriting the car's speed. Timeout/exit/space loss detaches safely without invented damage, points or restored allowance. No valid detachment means a safety failure to contain and fix, not permission to teleport through solids.

**Battery upgrades.** Big battery increases capacity; Fast charge increases the refill rate; Kinetic charge reads eligible pre-bonus impact damage and cannot refill a body already written off. Their legacy numerical effects are candidates, not an approved price/value proposition. The slice does not sell these upgrades.

All qualifying player hits share the declared player-hit learning rule. Cones and other environmental interactions intentionally do not train. Future exceptions require an explicit profile revision, not an inferred approach classifier.

### 5.9 Tuning method

Use immutable base data plus owned upgrade IDs to derive effective values. Log configuration identity, action parameters, purchases and outcomes. Compare matched input traces for mechanics and counterbalanced human trials for comprehension and choice. Change one parameter or one explicitly coupled group at a time and record why.

Information upgrades cannot be validated by an omniscient bot. Numerical advantages do not prove pleasure or a meaningful choice. If the baseline control scheme changes, re-evaluate its dependent upgrades and cues together.

## 6 The Fleet — behaviour contracts

### 6.2 Sensing, intention and commitment

Vehicles sense using a forward rectangle starting at the nose, with length given by the learned snapshot and width equal to vehicle width plus the 26-unit dummy diameter. Side sensors originate at the nose, reach 100 units and use an 80-degree half-angle about each lateral axis; their detection requests slowing, not an independent swerve. Reaction delay is 0.15 s. These are trial geometry/response definitions. Occlusion is tested along the nose-to-target sensing ray against explicit masks. The player's sensing wedge is a separate system. World data explicitly states which objects block each sensor and which obstruct the player's optional diagnostic view. Being outside the player's wedge must never hide an actor, an essential threat cue, a fixed/expiring/interrupted route or a spent appearance.

The fleet may detect the dummy, plan a manoeuvre and then commit. These are different states with different cues:

| State | Behaviour and visible promise |
|---|---|
| Sensing | The dummy or prop is detected; no fixed route is promised |
| Intention | A candidate manoeuvre is readable but may still change |
| Committed | Traffic has approved a swept corridor and planned speed trajectory for at least 0.75 s; the fixed-route cue appears |
| Interrupted | An unforeseen obstruction forces safety braking; the commitment cue visibly breaks and an interruption cue replaces it |
| Released | The promised window has finished; a new intention/commitment may begin |

Commitment never promises a lethal hit, a minimum assessment or even contact. It promises the advertised corridor and speed trajectory until its recorded expiry, unless a safety interruption is visibly announced. Maintain the trial 0.75 s reservation on a rolling basis where traffic permits. If it cannot be extended and less than the current lunge-plus-recovery duration remains, show a distinct expiring cue rather than a fresh closed cue. Input remains permitted, but the presentation no longer implies a full action window. The 0.75 s minimum exceeds the base lunge plus recovery window of 0.65 s. It is a **Trial** minimum, not a promise that any position is hittable. During commitment the car cannot invent a new evasive route because the dummy moved. Traffic reservations include the footprint along the entire planned corridor and its speed trajectory. Ordinary post-commit player detection/side-sensor requests cannot silently change the reserved speed. A physical safety obstruction may trigger braking only with the interruption cue. Intended player interception remains an assessed collision if it occurs; detection alone must not automatically cancel every bait. A braking exception must be visible, logged and counted in the commitment test. Silent rerouting is invalid.

The 0.75 s minimum is a prediction horizon, not automatic permission to release the corridor at 0.75 s if the vehicle has not completed the manoeuvre. Keep the corridor and cue binding until the reserved manoeuvre clears, or explicitly interrupt it. A late cone may make a reservation unsafe. The car brakes and declares interruption; it does not drive through the cone or secretly steer around it. After the old cue ends, traffic may approve a new manoeuvre. Routine scenes must not depend on repeated interruptions to remain safe, since that would destroy the player's reason to trust the cue.

### 6.3 Traffic and recoverability

Traffic uses a deterministic fictional keep-right convention, authored lanes, explicit entrances/exits and stable arrival ordering. It must reserve space before admission and before a manoeuvre, use swept collision checks and maintain a legal stopping option. A blocked entry delays that scheduled appearance; it does not create another car, consume the opportunity or teleport the queued car inside.

Starting traffic settings are reaction delay 0.15 s, minimum following time 1.2 s and minimum clearance 8 units, acceleration 360 units/s², comfortable braking 400 units/s² and emergency braking 600 units/s². These **Trial** values are subordinate to safe footprint checks. They are not evidence that a particular arrangement of cones and cars is feasible. Class-specific departures must be declared in profile data and evaluated together with the commitment guarantee.

Requests are ordered by simulation arrival time, then stable IDs for exact ties; the old 0.5 s arrival-tie window is inactive. A 3 s wait without safe progress triggers deterministic recovery evaluation and the visible blocked-route prompt if recovery cannot proceed. A safe recovery remains collision-checked and must not silently despawn a car. The rear steering pivot starts 0.30 vehicle lengths behind the midpoint; full swept geometry, not pivot placement, establishes clearance. Entry requires a clear vehicle length plus 8 units and the ordinary reserved path. A recovery reverse is capped initially at one vehicle length per attempt. These are trial limits, not a liveness proof. Exits, stopped vehicles, late obstacles and multiple arrivals are required test fixtures. A car may wait safely, reverse or replan only through a legal, visible recovery manoeuvre; a new route cannot preserve a misleading old commitment cue. Normal play must not produce persistent jams. A prolonged blockage exposes its cause and offers Resume or End test. If safe recovery cannot restore play, end as an unranked technical interruption and reset cleanly. This is containment, and counts as a failure in the liveness suite. It must not award damage, learning, score, credit or success.

### 6.4 Cars learn only from player hits

**User decision:** cones do not train cars. A cone may divert or stop a vehicle, but that interaction never creates a learning update. A later positive-health-loss collision between that car and the dummy may train it solely because it was a player hit. No cone/direct/bait attribution or causal-encounter classifier is needed in V29.

Each unique first positive-health-loss player contact for an appearance increments its model's player-detection range by `learning.playerHitStep` of baseline, capped at `learning.playerHitCap`. At current values: base 320, +25.6 per applied update, maximum 448 after five updates. No minimum hit size applies. Zero-health-loss contact, repeated event, spent appearance, environmental/cone contact and an attempted update at cap do not train.

Only future appearances read the new model value; existing appearances keep their admission snapshot. Other parameters, including cone look-ahead, braking, speed and commitment timing, do not secretly learn. Cross-model sharing is off. This simple rule intentionally replaces V27's multi-approach adaptation; whether it produces a recognisable interesting response is an open human-playtest question.

With five possible effective updates, notches use applied-count thresholds 0/1/3/5 for 0/1/2/3 notches. Appearance tags display the admission snapshot; a report may show the separately labelled model record. The final threshold changes from six to five because six increments are no longer reachable in this single capped channel.

Log unique hit ID, appearance/model ID, health lost, old/new applied count and old/new range. A report must not claim the car learned from a cone or inferred strategy. The first explanatory adaptation debrief is withheld until the test observer has recorded a response or non-response during a changed appearance's detection/commitment encounter. The engine records `changedEncounterObserved`; the natural-visit test records the player's behaviour before explanation. Admission alone does not unlock the explanatory copy. Persist the disclosure flag for checkpoints.

### 6.5 Recurrence, moods and voice

The slice schedule is eight repetitions of **Sedan–Van–Sedan–Sedan–Van**: 40 possible appearances, 24 Sedan and 16 Van. The initial nominal admission interval is 12 active simulation seconds. The first due appearance is at stage start; legal admission can be delayed by an occupied entrance. Admission means creation at the validated 200-unit off-screen lead-in, not arrival at the visible edge. At unimpeded cruise, nominal lead-in travel is about 1.45 s for Sedan and 1.67 s for Van; turning, braking and initial footprint placement can add time. Scheduled, admitted and exited counts are distinct. A successful early ending cancels the unused remainder. No quota implies that all 40 must appear.

The schedule is deterministic and part of the versioned profile. For slot `i`, admission cannot precede `max(12 × i, previousActualAdmission + 12)` and still requires safe entry; the first slot has no previous-admission constraint. A due appearance waits for safe entry, avoiding a burst after a blockage. No opportunity disappears while queued. The last nominal admission is at 468 s (39 intervals), not 480 s. This is neither a minimum session length nor a ten-minute completion guarantee: success can cancel most of the queue. Interval, approach geometry and delay are jointly tested for ten-minute pacing and recurrence before minute five. In the unobstructed sequence, the next Sedan slot is 12–24 s later and the next Van slot 24–36 s later; the first new snapshot admitted after a learning event is the relevant return, not an already admitted car.

The same model should visibly respond to an earlier successful approach while the visitor is still playing. Standard mood removes a major confound. Additional moods are expansion content and must specify independent behaviour effects, legible cues and exact roster allocation or a declared statistical tolerance. A population percentage alone does not guarantee that a short run contains a mood.

Barks may express corporate confidence, embarrassment or bureaucratic blame. They cannot assert facts the simulation did not establish. The first report may state the observed impact and model identity, but it withholds the explanatory learned-counter message until the player has encountered the changed return. Evaluation asks the player what changed before supplying that explanation. Later reports may make the adaptation explicit.

## 7 Levels and Environment — contracts

### 7.2 Environment contracts

World objects declare collision, sensing and rendering separately. The active surface whitelist is tarmac and pavement, both with player/lunge/car speed and braking factors 1. Shoulders are pavement; kerbs and painted stripes add no implicit mechanics. The active structures/props are barriers, fixed crates and cones. Van, barrier and crate block car-forward, car-side and player-diagnostic rays; Sedan and cone do not. No mask suppresses essential actor/commitment/spent cues. These explicit trial masks replace the legacy height-threshold and shared occluder boolean. A solid barrier, a visual occluder and a gap are not interchangeable. A prop's visible footprint must agree with the collision footprint used for placement and traffic clearance. Cover blocks only its declared sensor channels; that must be observable in diagnostics and in the required encounter fixture.

The Van's height supports mobile occlusion, but its presence does not remove its own identity, spent-state or threat cues. Physics continues outside the optional diagnostic cone. The normal camera must show the full active Road; a UI redesign must not crop an approach without providing and testing an equivalent warning.

The slice contains no required holes, low-grip trap, multiplier plate or elevated route. Core manoeuvres must work on normal road before terrain modifiers are introduced. Moving a cone cannot seal every legal recovery route or permanently block the finite traffic schedule. Placement is rejected when the full footprint is invalid, with feedback that leaves the prop in the dummy's possession.

### 7.4 Expansion environments

Road, Junction and Roundabout remain the intended progression of traffic situations. The slice does not require all three. Each expansion stage needs a distinct tactical purpose, a stage quota, a finite opportunity budget and a tested upgrade-coverage matrix. Junction right-of-way and roundabout entry are explicit authored traffic rules; new intersections do not inherit a claim of safety from straight-road tests.

Terrain expansion retains pavement/kerbs, grass/hedges, gravel, puddles, oil, mud, launch mats, impact plates and speed bumps as candidate content. Each surface declares separate player movement, lunge, car speed, braking, sensing and score effects. A visual material cannot acquire implicit physics. Only profile-enabled surfaces affect a run. The legacy surface values require reconciliation and testing before promotion; they are not active slice settings.

An impact plate's proposed 1.5 multiplier applies to score only, never health loss or credits. A launch mat modifies the declared reach calculation and must use the same prediction renderer. Slippery terrain requires braking/commitment witnesses. Gap traversal requires Jump's full-footprint landing rules; Jump cannot bypass an ordinary solid wall by treating it as a gap. Falling must specify its recovery position, elapsed simulation cost, body consequences and finite-traffic consequences before being offered.

Height bands and sensor masks remain data fields so cover and future traversal do not require a rewrite. Multi-deck navigation, scrolling worlds, weather and fog are expansion-gated. They must preserve critical cues and give an actual tactical return for their production and testing cost.

## 8 Impact and Scoring

### 8.1 Contact episodes and the one-use allowance

A contact episode starts when the swept dummy and vehicle footprints first meet. It ends only after they have been fully separated for at least one simulation tick. Assess the impact at episode entry. Holding contact while a stopped car accelerates does not manufacture a new impact every frame. A new, separated encounter may be assessed if the appearance is still unspent.

The first **positive-damage** episode consumes that appearance's damage allowance. Zero-relative-closing-speed/zero-damage contact does not consume it. The vehicle stays physically solid after spending its allowance, displays a persistent non-colour spent cue, and cannot damage or reward another body during that appearance. It may still obstruct movement. A later appearance of the model has a fresh allowance.

All relevant contacts use swept checks. A fast lunge cannot tunnel through a vehicle. Resolve contacts by time of impact, then stable appearance ID for an exact tie. Sample zone, speed and action eligibility at that resolved contact. A lethal event makes the body unavailable for further reward-bearing contacts; later contacts in the same tick cannot create another write-off.

### 8.2 Damage uses actual relative closing velocity

**User decision:** actual motion replaces the fixed 5× lunge multiplier. A stationary car can be damaged against by a moving dummy; a fast co-moving or separating pair does not receive damage merely because one speed is large.

Let `n` be the unit contact normal pointing from the dummy into the vehicle. Sample world-space velocities immediately before physical response at swept contact time:

```text
closingSpeed = max(0, dot(dummyVelocity - carVelocity, n))
rawImpact = damage.baseImpact * zoneFactor * relativeMass
            * closingSpeed / damage.referenceSpeed
assessedImpact = min(rawImpact, damage.bodyHealth)
healthLost = min(remainingHealth, assessedImpact)
impactPoints = assessedImpact * scoreSurfaceFactor
remainingHealth = max(0, remainingHealth - healthLost)
writeOff = remainingHealth == 0
```

There is **no separate finishing-hit points threshold**. A hit assessed at 5 destroys a body with 5 or less health; it does not destroy a healthy body merely because it scored. Even a smaller positive assessment can finish correspondingly low health. Display rounding and score-only modifiers do not determine death. A minimum positive contact tolerance, if the actual solver needs one for numerical noise, must not silently become a gameplay finishing threshold.

The fresh-body health remains 800 and reference speed 138 as **unbalanced trial values**, not a preservation of V27 hit counts. Base impact remains 100. On a front contact, a stationary dummy versus a Sedan moving toward it at 138 assesses 100. A dummy moving into a stationary Sedan at 69 assesses 50. Early fresh lunge at 1,148 toward a Sedan approaching at 138 gives raw approximately 931.884, capped at 800: one impact can now complete a fresh body. Late-lunge 246 against that Sedan gives approximately 278.261. Those changes deliberately reopen balance and timing; the old two-front/six-flank progression estimates are retired.

| Contact zone | Factor |
|---|---:|
| Front | 1.00 |
| Front diagonal | 0.60 |
| Side | 0.30 |
| Rear diagonal | 0.15 |
| Rear | 0.05 |

Zone classification retains normalised car-local contact position and inclusive angular bounds 30/60/120/150/180 degrees from the nose. First containing bound wins. Degenerate centre contacts fall back to the opposite resolved contact normal; if still degenerate, classify side and flag invalid overlap. This geometric zone is separate from the physical contact normal used for closing speed.

Use binary64 internal arithmetic, stable evaluation order and unrounded totals. Display nonnegative values using nearest integer, half-up. Death uses unrounded health clamped to zero. Pure tangential motion has zero normal closing speed; spent contacts remain physical but reward-free. Final score still includes finishing excess once, capped by fresh-body health, plus the once-only write-off bonus. Human balance tests must assess speed-based impacts, stationary-car tactics and deliberate score detours.

### 8.3 Score and finishing incentives

Award impact points once per consumed positive-damage episode and a 2,500-point write-off bonus once per completed body. Impact points are based on assessed impact, not only remaining health. This intentionally rewards a strong finishing hit; it also creates a potential incentive to delay a finish, which T04 must investigate.

For example, with 100 health remaining and a 500 raw impact on normal ground, the body loses 100 health, earns 500 impact points and one 2,500-point write-off bonus. The 400 finishing excess is recorded for analysis, not added a second time. If this is the objective-completing body, it earns no spendable credit. The 800 assessment cap prevents arbitrarily large per-hit awards from future heavy vehicles or multiplicative effects.

Scoring only health removed would make a five-body win's ordinary damage-plus-bonus subtotal constant. V29 retains assessed-impact scoring while exposing its trade-offs. Compare score, actual health lost, completion and elapsed time together. Investigate sequences that farm score while avoiding the objective. No score multiplier changes learning credit, body count or purchasing currency unless an explicit future rule is approved and propagated.

### 8.4 Whole-tick outcome transaction

Removing a finishing threshold does not remove the accounting-order requirement. One coordinator stages all consequences before presenting any credit, report or ending:

1. Advance deadlines/refill to the tick timestamp, arbitrate input, and compute swept motion. Capture pre-response velocities and contact geometry at time of impact. Resolve physical contacts safely, including contacts that will award no reward.
2. Order candidates by time of impact, then stable appearance ID. Evaluate each against the **current staged accounting state**, not repeatedly against tick-start health. Keep physical snapshots immutable; refresh health/allowance/learning eligibility after each staged event.
3. Ignore already committed event IDs, zero-assessment reward attempts and spent appearances. On each qualifying contact, stage allowance, health, assessed points and player-hit learning. If health reaches zero, latch this body dead, stage one write-off and bonus, and ignore subsequent damage/rewards for this body while preserving physical safety.
4. After all current-tick contacts and appearance eligibility are settled, decide success, confirmed withdrawal or exhaustion in that order. Exhaustion means all scheduled slots admitted and every appearance spent or exited. A last tiny hit can win; a last lethal hit below quota ends as Failed test.
5. Only a nonterminal write-off grants a spendable credit. Publish the complete validated tick result atomically, through component owners, then send an immutable view to presentation. Terminal outcomes never briefly open a shop or expose a credit that is later retracted.

Duplicate delivery has no effects. If validation fails, publish no partial state. Required fixtures include two individually nonlethal hits jointly destroying one body, a 5-health/5-assessment finisher, last-hit success, last-hit failure, duplicate terminal delivery and an earlier zero-damage episode followed by valid separated contact. `v28_reference.resolve_tick` demonstrates accounting over supplied physical snapshots; a real swept solver is still required.

### 8.5 Write-off, cone conservation and replacement

Automatic idle repair remains disabled. After a nonterminal write-off, freeze the world for report/shop; identity, owned abilities, models, existing appearances and props persist. Finish current-tick physical resolution before removing the destroyed body's collider; wreck/debris animation is visual only and cannot become an undeclared traffic obstruction. A final write-off goes to results with no shop or replacement.

**User decision:** if holding a cone, release the same cone at the body's destruction position. Clear ownership and any B gesture atomically with the write-off. Re-enable its collider only if its full footprint is valid there. If occupied, try authored recovery points in the same permitted connected region, ordered by squared distance from destruction position then stable point ID. Visibly identify a fallback as recovery placement; do not pretend it landed at the death point.

If every candidate is occupied, preserve that same ID in `pendingRelease`, with its destruction position recorded and no collider; it is neither deleted nor available for pickup. Retry the same ordered candidates as space clears. No new cone is granted. Invariant during the stage: initial prop IDs equal the disjoint union of free/in-flight, held and pending-release IDs. No duplicate owners or replacements.

After report/purchase, choose the first legal full-footprint replacement position by authored priority then ID, including the next-tick swept clearance. If a body position or pending cone still cannot be resolved, enter `REPLACEMENT_RECOVERY`: disable admissions and rewards, keep the player/resource clocks paused, advance only existing traffic and recovery for at most `traffic.deadlockTimeout` simulation seconds, and retry legal placement each tick. Keep the blocked-state explanation visible. If unresolved, end as an unranked technical interruption with the intact prop ledger recorded; never delete an obstruction, overlap actors, fabricate a safe point or grant another cone. This is containment and a failed liveness witness.

If resolved, instantiate the replacement with effective full health/charge and resume normal admissions without a catch-up burst. Existing appearances keep allowance and learning snapshots. T07/T08 explicitly include death while carrying in a crowded lane, repeated replacements, all cone recovery points occupied, all body spawn points occupied, and recovery that later clears. Geometry validity comes from the authored level/engine, not from the reference fixture's test callback.

## 9 Economy — transactions and records

### 9.3 Shop and purchase transaction

The shop presents only profile-enabled nodes. Show cost, prerequisite, owned state, credit balance, a short action description and a preview of the changed control or movement. A locked follow-on names its prerequisite; an unaffordable node shows the exact shortfall. No decorative expansion node appears purchasable.

Purchases validate the current phase, node availability, prerequisites, ownership and balance, then atomically subtract credits and add the owned ID. Repeated activation cannot spend twice. Derive effective body values from immutable base data and the complete owned set; never stack an effect by replaying purchase messages. Commit the resulting values before returning to play.

Replacing a written-off body restores its health and charge to the effective new-body limits. Buying a future capacity upgrade between stages with a surviving body changes the limit and clamps existing charge; it does not grant a free refill unless the profile explicitly says so. Health and charge are not implicitly reset at every menu.

### 9.4 Records and eligibility

A score category identifies profile/version, effective gameplay configuration, applicable build compatibility, ending class and continuation status. Licensed completions, explicit Non-compliant withdrawals and failed tests are separate categories; an incomplete farming run must not displace a completed run. Within a comparable category, sort points descending, then active simulation time ascending. Retain total interaction time separately for experience analysis.

Fresh uninterrupted approved runs are ranked separately from continued runs. Practice and debug runs are unranked. Applying a tuning change or debug grant permanently invalidates that run's ranked eligibility, even if the value is reverted. Developer controls stay out of the visitor flow. Accessibility settings that do not change gameplay can remain in the same category; settings that change timing or physics require an explicit category rule.

Store configuration identity with each record. A changed profile archives or separates earlier scores; it does not relabel them as comparable. This is local score integrity, not a claim of cheat-proof security. Initials entry is optional and must not obstruct replay or the visitor reset.

## 10 The Run — phases, clocks and checkpoints

### 10.1 Phases and clocks

| Phase | Simulation | Available interaction and transition |
|---|---|---|
| Attract/start | No active run | Read short controls and contract; start fresh, or Continue only if a valid checkpoint exists |
| Active play | Advances | Move, face, lunge and use owned abilities; admission, learning and objective evaluation run |
| Pause/focus loss | Frozen | Resume explicitly; settings; truthful checkpoint/ending commands |
| Hit feedback | Frozen for declared hit-stop | Present an already committed outcome; do not commit it again |
| Write-off/report | Frozen | Show body result; continue to shop only if nonterminal |
| Shop | Frozen | Purchase or save; confirm return; replace body if due |
| Stage closing | Traffic/recovery only; Body frozen | Cut admissions, drain safely, then report/purchase/normalise into checkpoint phase |
| Ending/results | No advancing run | Show outcome, eligible record and replay/reset |

Gameplay clocks use simulation time during active play; stage closing/replacement recovery advance only traffic/recovery while Body/resource clocks pause (§§8.5/10.4). UI animation and the unattended-session watchdog use a separate real-time clock. Reports and shops contribute to measured total interaction time even though the world is frozen. Do not tune to ten minutes by omitting time spent in menus.

On focus loss, pause the simulation and require explicit Resume. In exhibition mode, 60 seconds without meaningful input triggers a 15-second visible resume warning, then an unranked abandonment reset. The watchdog covers pause, shop and results as well as active play. Filter controller drift so it does not hold a session open. Attendants can extend or disable the watchdog for access needs. It never awards Non-compliant status or submits an abandoned score.

### 10.2 Objective, finite opportunities and ending priority

At the start, show the contract: complete five bodies in this test. Keep body progress and the finite traffic budget readable during play. There is no secret sixth body and no forced ten-minute countdown. All 40 scheduled appearances are potential opportunities; the run ends early on success. Exhaustion is evaluated after all contacts in the current tick have been resolved: every scheduled slot has been admitted and every admitted appearance is either spent or has exited. If the objective is still incomplete, end as Failed test immediately; a spent car need not drive off-screen before a result can appear. Unspent zero-damage touches remain possible opportunities. Do not silently cancel queued slots or count spent appearances as exited in telemetry. A lethal last opportunity that does not complete the contract is therefore a terminal failed write-off: bonus and impact points are awarded, but no spendable credit, shop or replacement body is created. A temporarily blocked queue is not exhaustion.

For a terminal tick, resolve in this order:

1. Success earned by the last valid collision.
2. Explicitly confirmed withdrawal.
3. Exhausted opportunities without success.

Technical interruption and abandonment are separate unranked outcomes. Neither is authored as evidence that the player deliberately refused the institution. Refusal becomes available after at least one completed body and requires a second confirmation. Its ending is an intentional choice, not a punishment for standing still.

| Outcome | Meaning | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed | Displayed contract completed | Closure, score and invitation to try another opening |
| Non-compliant | Player explicitly refuses continued testing | Authored refusal ending and separate record category |
| Failed test | Finite useful traffic is exhausted before completion | Clear result, explanation and quick retry |
| Abandoned | Visitor leaves or chooses to end without an authored refusal | Reset without leaderboard submission |
| Technical interruption | The system cannot continue safely/correctly | Explain briefly, log cause, reset unranked |

### 10.4 Stage closing and checkpoint continuation

The one-stage slice has no mid-run save. Pause offers Quit to last checkpoint with exact loss disclosed, or End run/Cancel if none exists. Save and quit is immediate only at `READY_FOR_NEXT_STAGE`; Continue is hidden without a valid compatible checkpoint. Terminal results create no continuation checkpoint.

For a nonterminal stage quota/final-condition event, first complete its entire collision tick under §8.4. Then enter `STAGE_CLOSING`:

1. Cut new admissions. Mark the remaining outgoing-stage slots explicitly `cancelled-stage-complete`, not admitted, exited or failed opportunities. This cancellation is permitted only after that stage's displayed contract is complete.
2. Freeze dummy controls, health, charge and refill-delay state. End transient action/gesture state. Resolve any held cone by §8.5. After the completed collision tick, visibly withdraw the test subject into the noninteractive report phase and remove its traffic collider; this is an explicit completed-stage transition, not an invisible escape during active play. Wreck/debris is visual only. If a new body is due, defer instantiation until the later replacement step.
3. Existing traffic drains under normal collision-safe traffic rules. Traffic and a closing timer advance; body/resource clocks remain paused. No damage, score, learning, credits or additional write-offs can be earned during closing. No new vehicle appears. Preserve physical safety and prop conservation. Existing cars must leave legally, not disappear to manufacture throughput.
4. Attempt bounded safe traffic recovery when blocked. If traffic/reservations/pending releases remain after `run.stageClosingTimeout` (trial 30 s), end as a technical interruption, without creating a checkpoint. This is a test failure, not a permitted routine shortcut.
5. Once clear, complete the single combined report/purchase flow, replace a written-off body if due, and normalise outgoing props into the outgoing-stage ledger before unloading that completed world. No props transfer or duplicate into the next stage; its own authored prop IDs are stage-qualified. Preserve surviving health/charge/refill delay; clear velocity, action, attachment and carried-prop state identically in uninterrupted/restored paths.
6. Enter `READY_FOR_NEXT_STAGE` before any incoming vehicle is admitted. Save a complete validated snapshot atomically. Restore into paused review; only explicit Resume begins the next stage. Mark continued runs separately for records.

Run elapsed time includes active traffic-closing/replacement-recovery time; body timers explicitly do not. Report/shop time is interaction time only. Record each phase duration so recovery cannot hide poor pacing. A remaining refill delay is measured in paused body-simulation seconds and resumes unchanged.

T09 uses a small synthetic two-stage contract fixture, not a second finished level. Independent expected surviving-body boundary: health 137, charge 1.25, refill-delay remaining 0.20, unchanged credits/score/learning, velocity zero, idle action and no carried/live/reserved entities. Draining traffic for two seconds must not refill or heal that body. Compare subsequent restored/uninterrupted actions against that expectation, not just against each other. Also test replacement-due state, blocked closing timeout, pending cone, purchase-once and incomplete content identity. Reject incompatible saves without partial load.

## 11 Game Data and Architecture

### 11.1 Versioned definitions and effective values

V29 describes required architecture, not inspected repository status. The supplied tuning catalogue is source material and is not proven runtime-connected. Historical Built labels in V26 are not verification of this rewrite. Integrate with the existing implementation after inspecting it; do not infer that a new engine or a broad rewrite is necessary.

Separate immutable base definitions, a versioned `RunDefinition`, mutable run state and presentation views. The run definition selects levels, roster, finite schedule, objective rules, content availability, prices, learning responses, parameter overrides and score category. Resolve one complete effective configuration when the run starts. Body and Fleet derive values from that configuration and owned/learned state through named functions. Renderers read those results rather than calculating their own reach, credit, seniority or damage.

```text
Base definitions + RunDefinition/version
    -> validated effective configuration + hash
    -> RunState (body, models, appearances, economy, objectives, schedule)
    -> ordered simulation events and atomic outcomes
    -> read-only presentation views, reports, records and checkpoints
```

Canonical IDs must be stable. Display names may be translated without changing IDs. Unknown IDs, duplicate IDs, invalid references, wrong types/units, negative prices or durations, impossible prerequisites and non-finite numeric values are hard failures. Reject invalid configurations atomically. A design-range warning may permit a clearly experimental unranked profile; it cannot turn a structurally invalid profile into a ranked run.

|---|---|---|

Dependencies are real: an offered upgrade changes controls, body derivation, layout coverage, learning opportunities, shop preview and tests. Fleet timing changes cue guarantees, collision opportunities and pacing. Agents working on those areas must share the same contracts. Presentation cannot mutate health to match an animation; Economy cannot independently count write-offs from sound events.

### 11.3 Required run and event data

A run records profile/version, build/schema compatibility, full effective configuration/hash, schedule/seed, level asset identity, phase, active and interaction times, body identity, owned IDs, credits, points, objective progress, model learning, appearance state, and eligibility history. Log gameplay-affecting developer actions even if reverted.

Impact records include event ID, body ID, appearance/model ID, contact episode ID, simulation time, zone, speed, action eligibility, raw/assessed impact, health lost, finishing excess, score modifier/points, allowance transition, contact evidence, player-hit eligibility, actual applied increment, write-off ID and terminal/nonterminal disposition. Reports and tests consume this same record.

Record scheduled, admitted, spent and exited opportunities separately. Model learning updates record effective before/after values, so a capped attempted update is not mistaken for seniority. Purchases record balance and owned IDs before/after. These logs support analysis; the visitor interface shows only the facts needed to decide and understand.

### 11.4 Checkpoint schema

At `READY_FOR_NEXT_STAGE`, save:

- Schema/build compatibility, profile ID/version, complete effective configuration and hash, level/content versions and score category.
- Next stage, schedule definition/cursor, random-generator state where used, objective progress, event/identity counters and committed transaction sequence.
- Body identity, surviving health, charge, remaining refill delay, owned upgrades, credits, points and completed-body count.
- Model learning values, applied-increment counts, identity state and any report-disclosure state needed to preserve the learning-observation rule.
- Active simulation time, measured interaction time, continuation status and permanent eligibility history.

No active cars, reservations or carried props cross this checkpoint. The stage-boundary routine resolves them before serialization, using the same rules on an uninterrupted transition. If a future profile carries those objects across stages, it needs a revised schema and equivalent deterministic restoration tests before release. If service is later enabled, its relevant timers/state also become mandatory; omission cannot silently reset them.

The save writer constructs and validates a complete candidate before replacing the previous checkpoint. The loader validates IDs, configuration identity, compatibility and phase before committing a restore. A partially loaded body with an old economy is forbidden.

### 11.5 Concrete adapter semantics, units and identity

All simulation positions/distances are **world units**, velocities **world units/s**, acceleration/braking **world units/s²**. Pixels belong only to rendering. With viewport `(x,y,w,h)`, use `scale = min(w/world.width, h/world.height)` pixels per world unit; centre the projected world with letterbox offsets, then draw `pixelPosition = offset + scale * worldPosition`. Input picking uses the inverse transform. Changing viewport size never changes physics, speed, reach, health or collision geometry. UI sizes separately use reference-canvas units and a UI scaling policy. `v28_reference.project_world` demonstrates the transform.

The versioned profile's `consumerContract.referenceFunction` names actual supplied Python functions where available: `movement_sample`, `effective_reach`, `lunge_sample`, `throw_launch`, `impact`, `learn_hit`, `resolve_tick` and `project_world`, among others. Functions execute fixtures and parameter perturbations. A null reference function explicitly means engine implementation is still needed, not that a demonstrated consumer exists. This is an executable reference specification, not an adapter secretly connected to a game repository.

Concrete boundaries: at each tick before input, Body reads current ordinary walk/run/turn settings; an already accepted lunge retains its reach/path/duration snapshot. World accepts a throw request with the Body release snapshot and captures range/duration; later edits do not alter the in-flight cone. Fleet captures perception/learning/traffic defaults at admission. Purchases add owned IDs atomically, then recompute effective stats from immutable base and ownership; they do not cumulatively reapply deltas. Fixed-tick rate, collision geometry, scoring rules, prices and objectives change only between runs. Test/debug edits are validated and queued atomically at these boundaries and permanently un-rank the run, even if reverted. Invalid edits leave prior state untouched.

Use profile IDs in formulas rather than independent literal constants. Radius edits affect both drop clearance and forward-sensor width; reach edits affect movement and prediction; reference-speed edits affect the damage denominator. The generated tables are examples of effective values, not alternate authorities. Reference checks deliberately perturb inputs to establish those dependencies.

Distinguish the **design fingerprint** from a **release/save identity**. The latter hashes canonical effective rules/values, the exact compatible build bytes, and a manifest of actual level/gameplay-content bytes with stable IDs, versions and SHA-256 hashes. Include all outcome-changing assets, scripts and content definitions; a level name or placeholder status is insufficient. `bind_release_identity` refuses missing bytes or required content. A geometry, content-version, build or gameplay-parameter change produces a different identity. The current profile has no supplied build/level bytes and therefore a null ranked release identity and no ranked approval. Checkpoints and leaderboard categories bind the completed release identity, never the design fingerprint alone.

Use 60 Hz as the trial fixed simulation rate. Sample piecewise trajectories at exact swept contact times, with timer deadlines applied at the first tick reaching them. Hit-stop/write-off presentations use real presentation time while simulation is frozen. Stage closing and replacement recovery have the explicit clock exceptions in §§8.5/10.4. The gameplay engine must implement these contracts and pass T08–T10; reference success is not engine success.
