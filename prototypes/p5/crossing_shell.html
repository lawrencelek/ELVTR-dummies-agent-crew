A 45° crossroads · four engineered options

Example only, not in the districts. A W–E road (priority) crossed by a 45° road, in game art with live P5 traffic. Road design guides want crossings at 90°, accept 75° and realign anything under 60°. A 45° four-way crossing is below every guide's minimum. These are the four standard ways out.

Each option runs its own traffic. Cars enter at its edges about every 1.6 s.

Measured (P5 driver, cars only, 180 s × 3 seeds)

Generated by node tools/crossing_report.js. Conflict points are the textbook counts: a four-leg crossing 32, two offset T junctions 22, a four-leg roundabout 8. Offset T pairs are reported 1.5–2 times as safe as crossroads for the same flow. One junction passes 22–25 cars a minute here: the lab holds new cars while an entry is blocked.

Sources

  • Iowa DOT Design Manual 6A-8: intersection alignment and realignment — 90° ideal, 75° desirable, realign below 60°; realign the crossroad, or offset it into two T junctions (left then right preferred).
  • FHWA Handbook for Designing Roadways for the Aging Population, ch. 7 — AASHTO accepts 60°; the Traffic Engineering Handbook asks for no less than 75°.
  • Essex Design Guide: junction types and design — right-left stagger preferred (keep left); 45° is the hard limit for a skewed junction.
  • WisDOT Facilities Development Manual 11-26: roundabouts — legs ideally at 90°; a roundabout can leave an existing skew in place.
  • ODOT, Safety of 4-leg vs T intersections — 32 conflict points become 22 at two offset T junctions; offset pairs 1.5–2 times as safe.
  • FHWA Resource Center: roundabouts — 32 conflict points at a four-way intersection, 8 at a roundabout.
